SVY21, Singapore's national map grid, gives every spot two numbers: metres east (E) and metres north (N). Its maths starts at an unmarked point at , . That point could have been called 0, 0. Instead it was given the numbers above, so every corner of Singapore gets positive numbers.

So the grid's real zero is to the south-west, where the two magenta lines cross. Singapore's own lowest numbers are E  on its western edge and N  at , its southern tip.

    And the original Null Island

    from SVY21's Null Island, as the crow flies

    Records that lose their position often end up here, at latitude 0, longitude 0: open ocean on the high seas, outside every country's waters. There is no land, but NOAA lists one weather buoy right on the spot, PIRATA station 13010, “Soul”. Natural Earth, a widely used free world map, even includes the island: a square of “land” of , labelled “Null island”.

    Fun fact: it isn't strictly in the Gulf of Guinea. By the International Hydrographic Organization's sea limits it is outside it, in the South Atlantic.

    And SVY21? Ask it for this spot's address and it answers E , N . On the way here its numbers ran off to infinity at 0°, 13°50′ E, a quarter of the way round the world from Singapore's central meridian.
