    How it is drawn

    Wind. NEA's 10-minute average wind at each weather station, fetched live and refreshed every 5 minutes. The wind at each hawker centre blends the three nearest stations, weighted by one over distance squared: their wind arrows give the direction, their speeds the speed.

    Plumes. Each runs straight downwind from its centre: a textbook Gaussian plume, as in the US EPA's ISC3 model, spreading as it would over a city in neutral air (Briggs's urban curves, tables 1-3 and 1-4). Its strength is the number of cooked-food stalls, as if every stall smelled the same. Winds under 1 m/s count as 1 m/s, as in ISC3.

    The edge of each plume is where its smell is as faint as a single stall's would be 200 m downwind in a 1 m/s breeze. We chose that level for illustration; each darker wash is about 3 times stronger than the one around it.

    Left out: centres still being built, and markets with no cooked-food stalls.
