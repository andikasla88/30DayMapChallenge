Only buildings cast shade here, at approximate OpenStreetMap heights. Trees and awnings are left out, so real streets are often shadier.

How it works and sources

Each building's shadow is its footprint swept away from the sun by its height ÷ tan(sun's altitude). Heights are OpenStreetMap's mapped height, or floors × 3.66 m, or 5 m when neither is mapped. Raised parts such as link bridges are treated as solid down to the ground, and roofs can fall in the shadow of taller neighbours. The share in shade counts every second screen pixel of street and footpath in the open part of the map: OpenStreetMap streets and footpaths outside tunnels and expressways, drawn at typical widths (footpaths 2.5 m, service roads 5 m, side streets 7 m, main roads 10–20 m, each side of a dual carriageway half that), leaving out anything under a roof. Only buildings in the map tiles already loaded are counted, so a distant tower's long morning or evening shadow can be missed. The sun's position is NOAA's solar calculation for the centre of the map, in Singapore time (UTC+8).
