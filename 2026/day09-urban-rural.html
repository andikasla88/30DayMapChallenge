The densest Yishun East

77,840 people on 1.77 km²

Singapore's most crowded subzone is denser than Manhattan (28,870) and Dhaka South (39,400), and level with Mong Kok's district, Yau Tsim Mong (44,460).

It is not alone. 36 subzones are denser than Manhattan, and a third of all residents live in them.

The empty half under 300 per km²

63%of the land, home to
0.18% of residents

The UN-endorsed Degree of Urbanisation calls land rural below 300 people per km². By that line 100 subzones (green) are rural: 497 km² holding just 7,590 residents.

Most of it is not farmland. It is reservoir catchment, army training ground, airbase, port and factory.

Field trip 1 Lim Chu Kang

20residents on 17.5 km²
1.1 per km²

Singapore's farm belt: vegetable plots, goat sheds, frog ponds and fish farms on the Kranji countryside. It looks rural and counts as rural, but hardly anyone lives here. Most of the people who work these farms go home to the HDB towns.

Even this is temporary. The Singapore Food Agency plans to rebuild Lim Chu Kang as a high-tech agri-food zone.

Field trip 2 Sungei Tengah

8.6residents per km²
590 people in Murai subzone

Farm plots of the Sungei Tengah agrotechnology park, at the edge of the forested Western Water Catchment. On paper, Murai subzone is deep countryside.

But the resident count covers only citizens and PRs. Migrant workers in the dormitories out here, such as Sungei Tengah Lodge, do not appear in it. Rural by the table, not by the bus queue.

Field trip 3 Pulau Ubin

30residents on 67 km²
0.45 per km²

The North-Eastern Islands subzone (Pulau Ubin, plus the army island of Pulau Tekong) is home to 30 residents: sparser than Mongolia. Ubin's last villagers live in zinc-roofed kampong houses among the old quarry ponds. Everyone else arrives by bumboat from Changi Point.

Field trip 4 Lorong Buangkok

27,400residents per km² in
Trafalgar, the subzone around it

The last kampong on the mainland: about two dozen timber-and-zinc homes on roughly a hectare, hemmed in by landed estates, condominiums and HDB blocks. In the statistics it disappears. Its subzone, Trafalgar, holds 47,780 residents.

The most village-like place in Singapore sits inside one of its denser subzones.

Singapore's towns are as packed as Mong Kok and its empty corners are emptier than Mongolia. What it lacks is the village: rural Johor's 30 to 300 people per km². The few subzones at that density are Raffles Place, the Istana, NUS and Changi airport: offices, campuses and runways.

So: yes, Singapore is still rural, if rural means empty. It is a countryside without country folk.
